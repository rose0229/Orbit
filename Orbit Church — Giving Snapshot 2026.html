Section 01

Executive Summary

The 10 findings that matter most — evidence-backed, no filler.

01

Healthy giving church — with significant concentration risk

Total all-time giving: $1,899,076 since August 2022. Monthly giving runs $37,000–$49,000 in normal months — strong for a church plant your size. But your top 5 givers represent 24.4% of all giving and your top 20 represent 50.6%. Two or three household changes in your top tier would meaningfully impact your monthly budget. Data

02

Your formal recurring base is weaker than it looks

You report approximately $14,000/month in recurring giving. PCO data shows only 31 active formal recurring schedules generating $7,824/month. The remaining ~$6,200/month comes from people who give consistently but manually — they are not automated. One missed Sunday, one life change, one distraction — and it stops. Data

03

271 people gave once and never came back

43% of all identified givers gave exactly once and stopped. That is 271 households who said yes — and then went quiet. At an average gift of $300, converting even 20% to repeat givers adds over $16,000/year. A simple 7-day personal follow-up system can double your conversion rate. Data Inference

04

281 lapsed givers are waiting to be reactivated

These are not strangers — 281 people gave at some point and haven't given in over a year. Before spending energy acquiring new givers, build a reactivation pathway. A personal pastoral check-in (not a fundraising ask) can reactivate 10–20% of them, recovering $15,000–$30,000/year. Data

05

Your year-end giving culture is genuinely excellent

December is your largest month every year without exception: $27,941 (2022), $170,798 (2023), $83,574 (2024), $120,365 (2025). Your campaigns — IYKYK, Don't Settle — work. They are named well, vision-forward, and they generate real results. Build the rest of the year around this same energy. Data

06

The Orbit Advancement Fund raised $634,309 — remarkable for a church plant

33.4% of all Orbit giving went to the Advancement Fund, not general operations. Your stated $35–45K/month includes campaign contributions. True operating giving (General T&O) has historically averaged closer to $23,000–$28,000/month in many periods. This matters for budgeting and what you tell elders. Data

07

Only 41% of your attendance gave in the last 90 days

163 people gave in the last 90 days — approximately 41% of your weekly crowd. That is actually above the national average (25–30% in most studies). But it means 59% of your weekly attendees have not given recently. The opportunity for discipleship into generosity is massive. Data Inference

08

New giver acquisition is slowing in 2026

You added 60 new givers in Q1 2024, 63 in Q4 2024, 57 in Q4 2025. Q1 2026: 40 new givers. Q2 2026 is tracking ~38. That is a 33% decline from your peak quarters. You are not replacing departing givers fast enough. Worth investigating whether this reflects attendance trends, outreach, or follow-up gaps. Data

09

Median gift of $102.50 tells the real story

Median transaction: $102.50. Average transaction: $301.00. The gap is caused by a small number of very large gifts pulling the average up. Your everyday giving culture sits around $100/transaction — meaning most people are giving occasionally, not weekly. This is a formation opportunity, not a failure. Data

10

The raw materials for a world-class generosity culture are here

The campaigns are working. The heart is there. The congregation responds when vision is clear. What is missing: systematic first-time giver follow-up, a recurring giving invitation cadence, a lapsed giver reactivation strategy, and a pastoral discipleship pathway that normalizes generosity as formation — not fundraising. Section 08

Communication Strategy

Pastoral, clear, compelling — not manipulative. Every touchpoint designed for discipleship, not extraction.

Weekly Offering Moment

Lead with vision, not apology. A rotating set of 30–60 second scripts — one per month, pre-written, vision-forward. The person leading it should never apologize for the moment.

"Giving is one of the ways we participate in the mission together. If Orbit has been meaningful to you, giving is a way to say — yes, I want more people to find this."

First-Time Giver Follow-Up

Day 1: automated receipt. Day 3–5: personal email from the pastor — vision-focused, no ask. Day 30: story email if no second gift. Day 60: gentle recurring invitation.

"[Name], I noticed you gave for the first time recently. I just wanted to say — thank you. Your generosity is part of what makes Orbit possible."

Recurring Giving Invitation

Quarterly — not a desperate ask. Frame it as an invitation to a better way of giving. Lead with the ease, not the obligation.

"If you give most months anyway, recurring giving just removes the friction. It keeps your commitment consistent even when life gets busy."

Lapsed Giver Reactivation

First contact is never about giving. It is pastoral. Check in. Ask how they are doing. The gift conversation follows the relationship — or it doesn't, and that's okay too.

"[Name] — I was thinking about you. How are you doing? We miss you. Is there anything Orbit can do to serve you or your family right now?"

Year-End Campaign Arc

Early November: vision email. Mid-November: brief top 20 givers personally. First December Sunday: campaign launch. Dec 15: story + one week left. Dec 28–31: final push. Jan 5: thank-you + impact.

Campaign names: short, vision-forward, specific. IYKYK and Don't Settle worked. Find the next one that fits the chapter God is writing.

Financial Transparency

Quarterly aggregate update to all attenders. Annual year-end summary. Total given, how it was used, vision for next year. Not a financial report — a celebration of stewardship.

"Here's what you gave. Here's what it built. Here's what's next." Make it a one-pager. Make it visual. Make it proud.
Section 09

Pastoral Discipleship Strategy

Generosity at Orbit is not a funding mechanism — it is a formation practice. This shapes everything.

Core Conviction

When someone gives, they are not just paying for ministry. They are practicing surrender, trust, and participation in God's mission. This distinction must shape how you talk about it, when you talk about it, and how you respond when people don't.

How Often to Teach It

FrequencyFormat
WeeklyOffering moment — 30–60 seconds, vision-forward
MonthlyOne email or video — generosity story or giving impact
QuarterlyA Sunday segment on stewardship or mission funding
AnnuallyFull 2–4 week series on biblical stewardship

Language That Works

DON'T: "Sign up for auto-pay" DO: "Set up automated generosity"
DON'T: "Give online" DO: "Give from anywhere"
DON'T: "We need your tithe" DO: "This is how we participate in what God is building"
DON'T: "If you don't give, we can't pay our bills" DO: "Your generosity makes [specific outcome] possible"

New Believer Pathway Into Generosity

At Salvation / Commitment
Introduce generosity as formation — not a rule, a rhythm
"As you grow in faith, you'll find that generosity is one of the most transformative practices." Frame it as invitation, not obligation.
First Month
Encourage any giving — even $5 matters as a practice
The amount is less important than establishing the habit. Generosity starts with the decision to participate, not the size of the gift.
90 Days
Begin the tithe conversation — as invitation, not demand
Tithing is an ancient and tested practice. Introduce it as "what many of our most grounded people have found — a starting point, not a ceiling."
6 Months
Invite to recurring giving — make faithfulness sustainable
"This is how many of us maintain our giving commitment even when life gets busy. It removes the friction and keeps the practice."
1 Year
Invite to a First Fruits class or stewardship experience
A 4-week formation experience for anyone wanting to go deeper in stewardship. Not a pledge drive — a discipleship journey.
