Our Foundation

More Than a Voice

"I appeal to you therefore, brothers and sisters, by the mercies of God, to present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God, which is your spiritual worship." Romans 12:1

At Orbit Church, worship is not a performance. What happens on a Sunday platform is simply an overflow of what has already been surrendered Monday through Saturday. This audition process is not designed to find the most impressive voice in the room — it is designed to find faithful people who are growing, teachable, and willing to lead others into genuine encounter with God.

What We Evaluate

Heart & spiritual posture, vocal ability and potential, commitment to preparation, coachability, and cultural fit with our team values: Authentic. Excellent. Engaging. Contagious.

What We Are Building

A multi-generational team of disciples who serve with skill and humility — including future worship leaders, songwriters, and young musicians discovering their gifts for the first time.

The Journey

Audition Process — Overview

The audition is broken into three phases over 1–3 weeks, depending on scheduling. This allows us to evaluate not just ability, but preparation, heart, and consistency — things you can't always see in a single audition day.

1
Phase One — Week 1
Video Audition
Submit a video of yourself singing a cappella in a comfortable key, choosing from our provided song list. The video should be clear in picture and sound, with a clean, distraction-free background. Once submitted, the link is shared with both worship leaders and the designated runner simultaneously.
  • Head shot or full body, camera facing you — no filters, no studio edits
  • Sing a cappella — wear earbuds and play an instrumental track through your phone if it helps you stay on pitch, but we only want to hear your voice
  • Sing at minimum a verse + chorus. Full song is fine. We just need enough to evaluate your voice.
  • No distracting backgrounds or noise — help the listener focus on your worship
  • On the audition form, indicate which tier you are aiming for: Lead Vocalist / Frontline Harmony / Choir & BGV. You may select more than one. This helps us tailor the feedback and next step.
  • Submit as an unlisted YouTube link. All three leaders (both worship leaders + designated runner) receive the link.
  • Video deadlines open in weeks 1–2 of each quarter — four cycles per year. Deadlines exist for a reason: they give people a reason to act on what they've already been thinking about doing.
Swift follow-up is non-negotiable. This is likely one of the most vulnerable things this person has ever done. Response within 24–48 hours. All three leaders are on the link so no one falls through the cracks. People remember their first interaction — make it count.
2
Phase Two — Week 2
In-Person Prep & Mics-Off Rehearsal
If invited forward, the vocalist receives a prep sheet from a worship leader — heart & culture questions, experience questions, and the songs we currently do as a church. This gives them time to prepare authentically rather than answer on the spot under pressure. A runner (Anna or Hanna) may facilitate this step.
  • Worship leader or runner walks the vocalist through the prep sheet in person and explains what to expect
  • Vocalist takes the sheet home, prays over their answers, and prepares their audition song
  • Where possible, invite them to stand with the team at rehearsal — mics off — before the audition
  • Ten minutes in rehearsal will tell you more about posture and engagement than any audition format
Heart Interview Questions (from prep sheet): These are not trick questions — frame them as a conversation, not an interrogation.
  • Share your testimony — how did you come to faith?
  • What does your current spiritual walk look like right now? (Bible, prayer, community)
  • Where do you see yourself in worship ministry in the next 2 years?
  • Looking at our three tiers — which one do you feel best describes where you are right now, and why?
  • Tell us about your previous church background. Have you served on a worship team before?
  • Is there someone from a previous church we could contact as a reference — a pastor, worship leader, or ministry leader who knows your character?
Purpose: Cast the vision and culture. Reduce audition-day nerves so they can worship freely and answer authentically. Showing up prepared demonstrates determination, commitment, and heart. The reference check gives you a window into character you cannot see in a single audition — use it.
3
Phase Three — Week 3
In-Person Audition
A structured three-part audition covering heart, practical singing, and a clear next-steps conversation. The singing portion is tailored to the tier the vocalist is pursuing — Lead vocalists get the full band experience at a quarterly rehearsal or Rally; Frontline and Choir are evaluated with piano or guitar only and do not require a stage. At least one worship leader (Isaiah or Brecklyn) must be present. A designated runner (Anna or Hanna) assists.
Part 1
Prayer & Heart
  • Open with prayer together
  • Ice breaker — make them feel welcome, not interrogated
  • Walk through heart & culture questions from the prep sheet
  • Experience background and previous church context
  • Reference check — confirm who we can contact and how
  • Listen carefully; annotate any concerns for leader review
Part 2
Singing Audition
Format varies by target tier
  • Choir / BGV: Melody check only — verse + chorus a cappella or with piano/guitar. No stage needed. Evaluate: pitch center, tone quality, posture.
  • Frontline / Harmony: Harmony check with piano or acoustic guitar only. Sing the melody, then ask them to find a high harmony and a low harmony. Can they hold it independently?
  • Lead Vocalist: Full band audition — scheduled at a quarterly rehearsal or Rally. Full song with the team on stage. Evaluate: presence, pitch under stage volume, communication, worship freedom.
  • Across all tiers: evaluate recovery — how do they respond to a missed note or an unexpected change?
  • Confidence check: do they worship freely or do they shut down under observation?
Part 3
Wrap-Up
  • Encouragement first — name at least one specific thing they did well
  • Explain the next steps and timeline for a response (24–48 hrs)
  • Open the floor for their questions
  • Close in prayer if appropriate
Placement

Vocal Tier System

Tiers are not permanent labels — they are starting points. A vocalist placed in Choir can grow into Frontline through the Formation Season and beyond. The rubric score and the harmony check together determine placement.

Tier 1
Choir / BGV
Score 55–70 · Harmony <3
  • ✓ Strong heart and worship posture
  • ✓ Solid pitch and tone in melody
  • ✓ Harmony not yet consistent
  • ✓ Background or choir-level role
Sings with the choir or background ensemble. Does not carry individual mic lines on stage.
Tier 2
Frontline Vocalist
Score 55–74 (harmony ≥3) or 75+ (harmony <4)
  • ✓ All Choir criteria, plus…
  • ✓ Can find and hold a harmony (high or low)
  • ✓ Stage presence and blending ability
  • ✓ Supports the lead — not yet leading
On stage with a mic in a support role — harmonies, BGV parts, and response lines.
Tier 3
Lead Vocalist
Score 75+ · Pitch ≥4 · Harmony ≥4 · Confidence ≥4
  • ✓ All Frontline criteria, plus…
  • ✓ Worships freely without being prompted
  • ✓ Finds harmony both high and low with ease
  • ✓ Can hold the room when the lead needs support
Carries lead or co-lead vocal lines. Capable of stepping up in a service when needed.
⟳

Not Ready Yet — Score Under 55

This is not a closed door. The vocalist receives detailed written feedback on specific areas to grow, an encouragement, and an invitation to re-audition in the next quarterly cycle. Our job is to walk with them — not just evaluate them.

Next Step

Formation Season — 8 Weeks

Acceptance into the team begins a Formation Season — not a probationary period, but a season of intentional growth and belonging. We are not just adding a voice to a playlist. We are welcoming someone into a family.

Weeks 1–2 · Observe & Belong

  • Attend all rehearsals — no microphone
  • Stand with the team on stage during service
  • Learn the culture, the rhythms, the people
  • No pressure to perform — just be present

Weeks 3–4 · Rehearsal Mic On

  • Full participation in rehearsals with mic
  • Mics-off on stage during service
  • One intentional piece of feedback per week from worship leader
  • Begin building trust with the team

Weeks 5–6 · Service Mic On

  • Sing in their tier role on Sundays
  • Support role — not leading yet
  • Worship leader observes: punctuality, posture, team engagement
  • How do they handle a hard Sunday?

Weeks 7–8 · Evaluation

  • Brief 1:1 with worship leader — affirm growth
  • Identify their next step and any growth areas
  • Decision: full membership, extend season, or redirect
  • Celebrate regardless of outcome

What We Watch During Formation

Attendance consistency · Coachability and growth · How the team experiences them · Whether they are the same person in week 1 as in week 8. Missed rehearsals extend the season — they do not automatically disqualify.

Tier Growth Is Possible

A vocalist who enters as Choir / BGV can grow into Frontline or Lead during or after Formation Season. There is no ceiling — only the next step. The worship leader team reviews tier placements at the end of each quarterly cycle.

Ongoing

Leadership Rhythms

The audition process is only as healthy as the team running it. These rhythms keep leadership aligned, ensure nothing falls through the cracks, and give the team a regular space to process and improve.

📅
Monthly Leadership Check-In
First Saturday of every month — worship leadership team meets together. This is not a rehearsal. It is a temperature check: who is thriving, who is struggling, what's coming up on the calendar, and whether any audition follow-up is needed. Consistent. Non-negotiable.
🎙️
Quarterly Audition Review
At the close of each audition cycle, the full leadership team (Isaiah, Brecklyn, Anna, Hanna) debriefs the process: What worked? What didn't? Any rubric calibration needed? Any Formation Season members ready to advance? Keep the process improving.
🤝
Formation Season Check-Ins
Every volunteer in Formation Season should have intentional touchpoints — not just when something goes wrong. A quick check-in at week 4 and a formal evaluation conversation at week 8. Make it feel like investment, not inspection.
Who Does What

Audition Team Roles

Every audition should have at least a worship leader present and a designated runner. No single person carries the whole process.

🎤
Worship Leaders
Isaiah & Brecklyn
  • Own and oversee the entire audition process
  • Conduct or attend the in-person audition
  • Make final tier placement decisions
  • Deliver feedback to all applicants (accepted or not)
  • Lead Formation Season check-ins and evaluations
📋
Runners / Helpers
Anna & Hanna
  • Receive and organize video audition links
  • Schedule in-person auditions and send reminders
  • Distribute and collect prep sheets
  • Take notes during in-person auditions
  • Handle follow-up communications
🎵
Prospective Members
That's You
  • Submit video before the quarterly deadline
  • Come to each step prepared and prayerful
  • Be honest and authentic — not impressive
  • Ask questions when you don't understand something
  • Trust the process, even if it takes more than one try
A Word Before You Begin

We Are Rooting For You

Whether you are brand new to singing or have been doing this for years, we want you to know that this process is not about filtering people out. It's about finding the right place for every person who wants to serve — and then walking with them as they grow into it.

If you feel something stirring in you — take the step. The worst thing that happens is you grow. We look forward to hearing your heart, and your sound.
